Development SOP

A complete process is a firm, one live deal, capital, a paid bench, and a finished project. The playbook is the knowledge. The software spec is a tool that is not built yet. This page keeps the lots honest until that tool exists.

How we get a complete process.
  1. Confirm the firm packet: legal entity, bank account, books, and insurance. Pro formas name Paul Bianchi, CEO. Those four documents are not in these folders, so this desk does not treat the firm as proved.
  2. Pick one site that already has a street address. Run only that lot through the eleven gates below. A status word does not close a gate. A named file does.
  3. Build the first software piece only, the Site Screener. One address in. A sourced brief out, with a go or no-go, and the next studies. A price appears only when a source states it.
  4. You hire the bench the brief flags: attorney, civil engineer, architect, general contractor, and CPA. You sign, you fund, and you file. The software does not.
  5. Close or kill that one deal, and keep the closing statement or the kill memo in the folder. That file is the track record. The next lot starts after it.

What has to exist

Firm

Entity, bank, books, insurance. Name on the pro forma is not the packet.

One real deal

One site under contract, with numbers from files. Thermalands has a land closing statement dated 19 Mar 2025. Other escrow and construction labels still need the contract or the permit.

Capital

Your equity plus a lender or investor commitment. A capital ask on a portfolio card is not a commitment.

Paid bench

Attorney, civil, architect, GC, and CPA, engaged in writing. Engagement letters are not in these folders.

Track record

A finished project with the closing statement in the folder. A closed-page ROI line is not that file.

The screener can shorten the research on step two. It cannot supply capital, a license, or the person who signs.

Required process

1. Strategy

Before a lot: asset, build-to-sell or hold, geography, and the return hurdle.

2. Source the lot

Write where it came from: broker, owner, auction, or assemblage. Record the address and APN.

3. Why it is vacant

Name the reason: shape, access, utilities, environment, title, demand, or off-site cost. That reason is the discount or the reason to walk.

4. Zoning

General plan, zoning district, allowed use, density, and the path: by-right, CUP, variance, rezone, or map. Get a zoning letter or a pre-application note.

5. Environment

CEQA path, Phase I, flood, fire, wetlands, and a geotech scope. A fatal flag with no priced fix stops the lot.

6. Contract

PSA or option, diligence period, deposit, and whether close depends on entitlements. In escrow counts only with an escrow number.

7. Diligence

Title, survey, will-serve letters, access, fees, a yield study, and a pro forma rerun on real numbers. If the spread fails, renegotiate or walk.

8. Entitlement and map

Application, conditions of approval, tentative map, then final map: surveyor, bonds, taxes, CC&Rs. Building permit is the single-lot version of this gate.

9. Capital and land close

Equity in first. Land loan only after the contract. Construction loan after plans, budget, and permits the lender named. Closing statement goes in the folder.

10. Construction

GC, license, contract form, permit numbers, monthly draws, inspections, lien waivers, then certificate of occupancy.

11. Exit

Sale, refinance, or hold. Closing statement or refinance note, distribution record, and the entity closed or kept on purpose.

Pass or stop

  1. Is the use allowed, and is any hearing risk priced and timed?
  2. Does the yield study hit the unit count in the pro forma?
  3. Is there legal access and utilities at a known cost?
  4. Is there a fatal environmental, soil, flood, or species issue?
  5. Is title clean, and do recorded restrictions block the plan?
  6. What are the government fees and off-site obligations?
  7. Does the land price leave margin after those costs?
  8. Does the exit still work if costs rise and sales slow?
  9. Is the capital committed, and who guarantees it?
  10. Can the loss at this gate stay inside the budget for this gate?
